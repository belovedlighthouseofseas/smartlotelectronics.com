An electronics shop with one weird policy: we test the things we sell.

Our story

Smart Lot started in late 2022 in a 400-square-foot garage in San Diego with three crates of returns-lot smart bulbs and a spreadsheet. We were trying to outfit a rental property without going broke, and we discovered something annoying: the "best" hardware on every review site was almost never the hardware those reviewers actually used at home.

So we started buying lots ourselves — manufacturer overstock, retail returns, the kind of inventory that has a story behind why it's discounted — testing each batch in real homes, and reselling the ones we'd actually keep. Three years and seven test homes later, we still operate the same way. The garage is bigger now. The spreadsheet is a database. The principle hasn't moved.

What we believe
The best electronics review is the one written by someone still using the thing a year after they bought it.
No commissions We don't take affiliate revenue, ad money, or co-marketing from any manufacturer we list. Period.
No paid placement The catalog order reflects what we'd buy ourselves, not who paid for the spotlight.
No theater Reviews aren't "unboxed in 48 hours." Minimum 90-day burn-in before anything gets listed.
The process

From auction lot to your door, in four steps.

STEP 01

Source

We buy from manufacturer overstock, returns lots, and pre-release test units. Never gray-market imports, never pulled-listing inventory, never anything with a sketchy paper trail.

STEP 02

Stress-test

Every unit gets a power cycle, firmware audit, and the specific benchmarks we'd lie about if we were the manufacturer. About 18% of what we buy never gets listed because something failed.

STEP 03

Live-in

The remaining units go into one of seven test homes for ninety days. Wi-Fi 6 mesh, plaster walls, kids, pets, the works. Failures get logged, not edited out.

STEP 04

List honestly

If we'd buy it again, we say so. If we wouldn't, it doesn't get listed. If there's a catch — a missing feature, a flaky integration — it goes on the product page in plain English.
