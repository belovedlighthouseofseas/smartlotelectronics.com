The Matter standard, explained without the marketing.

Three years in, "Matter" still means different things to different vendors. Here's what actually shipped, what's still vapor, and which hubs are worth your $129 today.

If you've shopped for a smart hub in the last eighteen months, you've seen the Matter logo on roughly everything. You've also probably noticed that two devices with the same logo still won't talk to each other half the time. That's not your imagination — the gap between "Matter-certified" and "actually interoperable" is real, and most reviewers won't say so because their affiliate links want the sale to go through.

The short version: Matter solved the boring problems first. It's good. It's also not yet the universal solvent the keynote slides promised.

What Matter actually fixes

Three things, mostly:

  • Onboarding. Scanning a QR code and getting a light bulb into Apple Home, Google Home, and Alexa simultaneously used to be a four-app exercise. Now it's one scan.
  • Local control. Matter-over-Thread devices respond inside your house, even when your internet is down. This is the upgrade nobody in marketing emphasizes because it doesn't sell subscriptions.
  • Cross-platform basics. On/off, dim, color, sensor read — the common-denominator features work across every major ecosystem.

What it doesn't fix (yet)

The headline gap is anything device-specific. A robot vacuum's no-go zones, a thermostat's learning schedule, a camera's recording rules — none of that is in the Matter spec, which means the manufacturer's own app is still the place those features live. Buy a Matter robot vacuum thinking you'll control everything from Apple Home, and you'll spend the first week disappointed.

The honest answer

Matter today is great for lights, plugs, sensors, locks, and basic thermostats. For anything with a screen, a motor, or a subscription business model, you'll still be opening the maker's app.

The cloud-dependency thing

Matter-over-Wi-Fi devices technically don't need the cloud, but most manufacturers still route through it because their app needs it. Matter-over-Thread devices route through your local Thread border router (your hub, your Apple TV, your Google Hub) — and they actually keep working when your ISP goes dark. If you care about your smart home outlasting your internet contract, that distinction matters.

Hubs worth buying in 2026

We've put 47 devices on each of the four hubs below, across three test homes, for at least 90 days. Here's our short list.

  1. Aurora Hub Pro — $129. Matter, Zigbee, Thread, local-first. Our default recommendation. The iOS app is mediocre, the rest is excellent.
  2. Apple TV 4K (3rd gen) — $129. If you're an Apple household already, you've been running a Thread border router this whole time and didn't know it.
  3. Home Assistant Green — $99. For people willing to read documentation. The most flexible option by a wide margin.

Hubs to wait on

  • Anything sold as a "Matter bridge" that only translates to a vendor cloud. Read the fine print.
  • First-generation Thread border routers from 2022. Performance is poor and most have stalled on firmware.

The buying questions to ask

Before you click checkout on any hub, ask these four:

  1. Does it run Matter and at least one legacy protocol (Zigbee or Z-Wave)? If you're starting from scratch and only need Matter, this matters less — but most homes have older gear.
  2. Does it support Matter-over-Thread, not just Matter-over-Wi-Fi?
  3. Will scenes and automations execute locally? Ask the vendor in writing if it's not on the box.
  4. What happens when the vendor decides to discontinue the product line? Is there a path to keep it working?
One more thing

The number of "Matter-certified" devices on Amazon outpaces the number that have actually been audited. Check the CSA database for the cert before you trust the box.

Read the spec, ignore the keynote, and buy the cheapest hub that does what you need. Matter is a real improvement — just not the universal one the marketing claimed.
